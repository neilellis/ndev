# Heuristics and laws

The UX pages use a small set of well-known rules. They apply on a phone, a tablet and a desktop. Only the way you apply them changes.

<Rule>
This section is a recommendation. It is not taken from the website. The examples come from the website. The rules come from Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics and Jon Yablonski's Laws of UX.
</Rule>

## Nielsen's ten usability heuristics

| # | Heuristic | On the {{name}} website |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Visibility of system status | The menu button sets `aria-expanded`. The feedback form says “Capturing page… This can take a moment.” See [Feedback and performance](/ux/feedback-and-performance). |
| 2 | Match the real world | Plain British English. No internal names. See [Voice](/voice). |
| 3 | User control and freedom | Escape closes the menu and the globe's concept pane. The motion toggle stops the globe. |
| 4 | Consistency and standards | One dark button style, one text link style, one focus ring on every control. |
| 5 | Error prevention | Email fields use `type="email"`, so a phone shows the email keyboard. |
| 6 | Recognition rather than recall | Every section starts with a numbered eyebrow, so the reader knows where they are. |
| 7 | Flexibility and efficiency | Keyboard users can move through the {{product}} tabs with the arrow keys, Home and End. |
| 8 | Aesthetic and minimalist design | Flat, ruled layout. One accent colour. One dark button per view. See [Visual hierarchy](/ux/visual-hierarchy). |
| 9 | Help users recover from errors | Errors say what to do next: “Enter a valid email address, or clear the Email field.” |
| 10 | Help and documentation | Concepts link to the essay that explains them. |

## Laws of UX

| Law | The rule | Read more |
|---|---|---|
| Fitts's law | Big, near targets are fast to hit. Make frequent actions large and close. | [Input and touch](/ux/input-and-touch) |
| Hick's law | More choices take longer to decide. Show the few choices that matter now. | [Progressive disclosure](/ux/progressive-disclosure) |
| Jakob's law | People expect your site to work like other sites. Use familiar patterns. | [Navigation](/ux/navigation) |
| Miller's law | People hold about seven items in working memory. Group long lists. | [Visual hierarchy](/ux/visual-hierarchy) |
| Gestalt grouping | The eye groups items that are close, alike or inside one boundary. | [Visual hierarchy](/ux/visual-hierarchy) |
| Aesthetic-usability effect | People think an attractive design is easier to use. Polish does not fix a real usability problem. | [Visual hierarchy](/ux/visual-hierarchy) |
| Doherty threshold | Give feedback within 400ms, or people think the system is slow. | [Feedback and performance](/ux/feedback-and-performance) |
| Peak-end rule | People remember the worst moment and the last moment. Take care with errors and confirmations. | [Feedback and performance](/ux/feedback-and-performance) |
| Serial position effect | People remember the first and last items in a list. Put the key items there. | [Navigation](/ux/navigation) |
