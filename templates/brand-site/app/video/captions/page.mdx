# On-screen type and captions

Film type is set for a 1920×1080 frame seen on a laptop or a meeting-room screen. Captions are burned in, because most people watch with the sound off.

## On-screen type

| Role | Style |
|---|---|
| Headline | Source Sans 400, 68–132px, −0.045em, line height 1.02. Emphasis in Source Serif italic, blue (sky on dark) |
| Eyebrow | 22px semibold capitals, +0.14em, number in blue with a 26px gap |
| Lead paragraph | 29px, line height 1.4, `--slate-700` |
| Body and lists | 25–32px. Never below 26px for anything the viewer must read |
| Labels and tags | 18–20px semibold capitals, +0.08–0.14em |
| Small print (essay dates) | 18px, muted. Decorative only |

The headline repeats or sharpens the narration. It never says something different.

| Position | Centred, 54px above the bottom edge, at most 1500px wide |
| Safe area | Keep the bottom 150px of every scene clear of content |
| Fade | 0.12s in, rising 8px; 0.12s out |

## Splitting captions

Captions are made from the narration’s word timings:

1. Break after a full stop, colon or question mark.
2. Break after a comma once the phrase has three or more words.
3. Split any phrase longer than **seven words** into even parts, so no caption is left as a single word.
4. Each caption shows from its first word until the next caption’s first word.

Captions show what is written on screen, not how it is spoken: the narrator says “{{domainSpoken}}”, the caption shows **{{domain}}**.

## Checks

- No caption covers on-screen content.
- Captions match the narration word for word, apart from the written forms above.
- British spelling in every caption.
