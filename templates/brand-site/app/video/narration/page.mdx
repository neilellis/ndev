# Narration

A deep, calm British voice. It sounds like a trusted adviser, not an advert.

## Voice

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Model | OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini-tts` |
| Voice | `onyx` |
| Word timings | OpenAI `whisper-1` |
| Tempo | 1.12× after generation |

Voice instructions, as sent with every line:

```text
Speak with a natural, educated British accent (southern English, received pronunciation).
Calm, confident, warm corporate narrator. Measured pace. Say '{{name}}' as '{{nameSpoken}}'.
```

The credits page says: “Voice — Generated with OpenAI text-to-speech.” Always disclose a generated voice.

## Writing for the ear

- One or two sentences per scene, about 8–30 words.
- Lead with the point. Put the strongest word at the end of the sentence.
- Use the colon for a turn: “It’s coordination: who decides, who answers for it, and whether anyone still knows why.”
- Lists of three, joined with “and”.
- No acronyms the listener has to decode, except AI.

## Write it the way it is spoken

| Write | Not | Because |
|---|---|---|
| seventeen | 17 | Text-to-speech reads figures unreliably |
| {{domainSpoken}} | {{domain}} | The voice must say “dot” |
| {{name}} (with the instruction above) | a spelling the voice gets wrong | The instruction fixes the pronunciation |

## Changing a line

1. Edit the line in `scripts/vo.json` and `script.md`.
2. Regenerate the voice for that scene only, then the timeline.
3. Check that the word cues still match words the narrator actually says. A missing word fails the render.
4. Listen to the whole film: tempo, pronunciation of names, and no clipped endings.
