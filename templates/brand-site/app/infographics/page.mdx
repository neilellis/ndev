# Infographics

An infographic shows a relationship or a measure as a picture. {{name}} uses few of them. The concept globe on the website and the evidence ladder in the film are the models. See [Diagrams and motifs](/diagrams) for the drawing rules.

<Rule>
The globe details come from `{{source}}/public/globe.js` and `public/styles.css`. The rules are a recommendation. Every figure in an infographic must come from the website, the essays or the film.
</Rule>

## When to use one

Use an infographic only if all of these are true:

1. The reader needs to understand a relationship, not look up a value.
2. The shape of the picture carries real information. If you remove the data and the shape stays the same, it is decoration. Cut it.
3. The detail behind the picture exists and the reader can reach it.

## Rules

1. **One idea per picture.** The globe shows how two families of concepts connect. It does not try to show anything else.
2. **Honest data only.** Every number, label and position comes from published material. Never invent a figure to make a picture work. If an example uses placeholder data, say so on the page.
3. **Every mark leads somewhere.** Each globe concept opens a pane with its description and a link to the essay.
4. **Colour always has a word.** Forest and rust tell the two families apart. They also have different shapes, round and diamond, and a written label.
5. **Use the tokens.** Every fill and stroke in an SVG uses a `var(--…)` token.
6. **Label on the picture.** Put labels next to the thing they name. Give a label that crosses the artwork a halo in the background colour. The globe labels use a paper-coloured `text-shadow`.
7. **Place by rule, not by eye.** Compute positions so the result is the same each time and labels do not collide.
8. **Motion adds nothing essential.** Everything must be visible and complete with reduced motion on. The globe is still and fully usable when it does not rotate.
9. **Accessible.** Give the picture a text alternative that tells the whole point in a sentence. Make every interactive mark work with the keyboard. The globe supports the arrow keys, Home and Escape.
10. **Small screens.** If the picture cannot shrink and stay readable, let it scroll inside its own container. The page must not scroll sideways.

## Do not

- Put a decorative picture beside a set of numbers.
- Use a picture that needs a legend before it makes sense.
- Hide the detail behind the picture so that it is slower to reach.
- Use 3D, gradients inside shapes or drop shadows on parts of a diagram.
