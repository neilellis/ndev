# Photography

Real places, real work, calm light. Photography shows the settings where organisational change happens: cities, public buildings, modern workplaces and people working together. It never illustrates “AI” literally.

## Choose

<DoDont
  good={<ul><li>City and civic settings.</li><li>Modern, uncluttered workplaces.</li><li>Calm, natural or dusk light; cool tones that sit with navy.</li><li>Clear space on one side for text.</li><li>People working, not posing.</li></ul>}
  bad={<ul><li>Glowing brains, robots, circuit boards, binary code.</li><li>Staged handshakes and pointing at screens.</li><li>Heavy filters, neon, lens flares.</li><li>Generic stock offices.</li><li>Recognisable people without a licence that covers it.</li></ul>}
/>

## Treat

- Put text over a photograph only with a navy scrim: `--scrim-photo-left`, darkest where the text sits.
- In film, photographs drift slowly (Ken Burns, scale 1 → 1.09).
- Product screenshots sit on sand `#f4f1e9` or the paper-to-sky gradient, with a 3px radius and a soft shadow.

## Source and credit

- Use Unsplash, or the client's own licensed photographs.
- Credit every photographer by name, both in the credits file and on screen (a credits page in film; a caption or footer on the web).
- See [Footage and credits](/video/footage) for the download and credit routine.

## Diagrams and products

Infographics and {{product}} screenshots are drawn, not photographed. {{product}} screenshots show **Example Corp, a fictional demonstration organisation**. Say so wherever they appear.
